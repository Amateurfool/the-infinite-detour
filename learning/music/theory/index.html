MUSIC & SOUND / TOPIC GUIDE

Music theory: the patterns behind the sound

Music theory is a way of describing how musical ideas are organised. It gives us a vocabulary for rhythm, pitch, harmony, melody and form — not a set of rules that music must obey.

Start with the building blocks

A piece of music can be explored through several connected dimensions. Rhythm concerns when sounds happen and how long they last. Pitch describes how high or low a note sounds. Melody is a sequence of pitches heard as a musical idea, while harmony describes how notes sound together.

Notes, scales and keys

A scale is an organised collection of pitches. In much Western music, the major and minor scales provide familiar foundations, while a key describes the tonal centre around which a piece tends to feel organised. Other musical traditions use different systems and concepts, so no single framework describes all music.

Chords and progressions

A chord combines notes, often built by stacking intervals. Chord progressions create movement and expectation: some changes feel settled, others create tension or suggest that the music is heading somewhere. The effect depends on style, context, voicing, rhythm and listening experience.

A practical way in

Choose a song you know well. Try tapping its pulse, identifying repeated sections, humming the main melody and noticing where the harmony changes. You do not need to name every chord to start hearing the structure.
