MUSIC & SOUND / TOPIC GUIDE

Audio technology: shaping and moving sound

Sound can travel through air, become an electrical signal, pass through circuits and software, and emerge from a speaker. Audio technology is the collection of tools and principles that make that journey possible.

The signal chain

An electric instrument produces a signal that can pass through cables, pedals, preamps, amplifiers and speakers. Each stage has an input level, output level and electrical characteristics. Understanding the order of the chain makes troubleshooting and creative experimentation much easier.

Effects pedals and processors

Effects change some aspect of a signal. Overdrive and distortion alter its waveform; compression controls dynamic range; chorus and delay create time-based variations; equalisation changes the balance of frequencies. Digital processors use algorithms to perform many of the same jobs in software.

Amplifiers and speakers

An amplifier increases the power available to drive a loudspeaker. The speaker converts electrical energy into movement, which creates pressure waves in the air. Cabinet design, speaker size, room placement and listening environment all influence the result.

Frequency and listening

Frequency is measured in hertz, while level is often described in decibels. A spectrum analyser can show how energy is distributed across frequencies, but measurements do not tell the whole story: listening conditions and human perception matter too.
