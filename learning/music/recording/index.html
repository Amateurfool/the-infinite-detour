MUSIC & SOUND / TOPIC GUIDE

Recording & production: from performance to track

A recording is a chain of decisions: capture a performance, turn it into a usable signal, shape and balance the sounds, then prepare a finished mix. Each stage has its own tools and creative possibilities.

Capture the performance

A microphone converts changes in air pressure into an electrical signal. An instrument can also connect directly to an audio interface. Microphone choice and placement, room acoustics, instrument setup and performance all influence what is captured.

The audio interface and digital audio

An audio interface connects microphones and instruments to a computer. Its analogue-to-digital converter samples the incoming signal and represents it as digital data. Sample rate describes how often it measures the signal; bit depth describes the resolution used for each sample.

Editing, mixing and effects

Editing organises and refines recorded material. Mixing balances levels, stereo placement and tone, often using equalisation, compression, reverb, delay and other processing. These tools can solve problems, create space or become deliberate parts of the sound.

The final track

Mastering prepares the completed mix for distribution and playback. It considers overall tonal balance, loudness, dynamics and the requirements of the intended format. A strong production begins long before mastering: the arrangement, performance and recording choices matter throughout.
