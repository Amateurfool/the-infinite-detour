Crazy golf: the good, the bad and the ugly

A look at three very different courses, from pirate-themed water hazards to safari obstacles and traditional seaside crazy golf.

Crazy golf is a simple idea with an enormous amount of room for imagination. A good course can turn a short game into a memorable outing through clever obstacles, unusual settings and holes that reward a little thought. This first instalment introduces three venues and records the factual details of their courses. The good, the bad and the ugly verdicts can wait until your own experiences and photographs are added.

Pirate Adventureland, Weston-super-Mare

Pirate Adventureland is a themed family attraction in Weston-super-Mare, Somerset. Its adventure golf course has 18 holes and uses a pirate setting, with the route taking players around themed obstacles and out towards islands reached by a water raft.

The venue’s own course description mentions a lagoon containing piranhas as part of the playful theme. It is designed as an accessible activity for a broad range of ages, with no advance booking required according to the venue’s published information.

Safari Adventure Golf, Hersham

Safari Adventure Golf is located on Assher Road in Hersham, Surrey. The course offers 18 holes, with an animal and wildlife theme that takes players through settings inspired by South America, Africa and Asia.

The course combines putting challenges with short wildlife facts at individual holes. Its published descriptions mention obstacles and animal-themed features including a monkey, lion, snake and flamingo. The venue also advertises food and drink on site, making it more than a stand-alone putting course.

Hastings Adventure Golf, Hastings

Hastings Adventure Golf is a seafront miniature golf complex in Hastings, East Sussex. It offers three separate 18-hole courses: a traditional Crazy Golf course, an Adventure Golf course and a Pirate Golf course.

The traditional Crazy Golf course uses familiar seaside obstacles, including a windmill, watermill, lighthouse and helter-skelter. The complex is also associated with the annual World Crazy Golf Championships, giving it a connection with the competitive side of miniature golf as well as family entertainment.
