02 / SPATIAL COMPUTING

Virtual reality, augmented reality.

Different approaches to blending the physical and digital — from complete immersion to information layered over the world we already see.

One important distinction

Virtual reality replaces much of the user's view with a digital environment. Augmented reality keeps the physical world in view and adds digital elements to it. Mixed reality goes further by allowing digital objects to appear anchored to, and interact with, the space around us.

03 / DIGITAL REHEARSAL

Learning by simulation.

Simulators can recreate situations that are expensive, difficult or dangerous to practise in the real world.

FLIGHT / 01

Flight simulation

From home flight simulators to professional training devices, combining cockpit controls, aerodynamics, navigation and visual environments.

DRIVING / 02

Driving and motorsport

Steering wheels, pedals, force feedback, physics engines and multi-screen or virtual-reality cockpits bring vehicle dynamics to life.

INDUSTRY / 03

Training and digital twins

Interactive replicas help people understand complex machinery, rehearse procedures and explore how systems behave under changing conditions.

DESIGN / 04

World-building tools

Real-time 3D engines let designers build, test and inhabit spaces before they exist as physical places.

04 / BODY & MOVEMENT

When movement becomes input.

Interactive systems need to understand what a person is doing. A growing range of sensors can turn movement, position and attention into usable data.

MOTION CAPTURE

Tracks the position and movement of a body or object, often using cameras, markers or inertial sensors.

HAND & GESTURE TRACKING

Recognises hand positions and gestures so that actions can be performed without a conventional controller.

EYE TRACKING

Measures where someone is looking, supporting interface control, accessibility and more efficient rendering.

POSITIONAL SENSORS

Use cameras, accelerometers, gyroscopes and other sensors to estimate position, orientation and movement.

05 / TOUCH & FEEDBACK

More than what we see.

Visual and audio feedback are only part of an interaction. Haptic technology adds touch, resistance and physical response.

TACTILE / 01

Vibration and texture

Controllers, phones and wearable devices use carefully timed vibration patterns to communicate events and sensations.

FORCE / 02

Force feedback

Motors and mechanisms can resist or move against the user, recreating steering forces, impacts and physical controls.

WEARABLE / 03

Haptic wearables

Gloves, vests and other wearable devices can provide tactile cues across different parts of the body.

ACCESS / 04

Accessible interaction

Touch-based signals and alternative controls can make digital systems usable in more ways by more people.

06 / SHARED ENVIRONMENTS

Worlds made to be inhabited.

Some interactive experiences are not simply played or visited once. They continue to exist, change and connect people over time.

PERSISTENCE / 01

Persistent online worlds

Shared environments that continue running when an individual player logs out, with economies, events and evolving communities.

NETWORKS / 02

Multiplayer infrastructure

Servers, latency, synchronisation and networking determine how a shared experience works across distant locations.

IDENTITY / 03

Avatars and presence

Digital representation, voice, gesture and spatial audio help people feel present with others in a virtual environment.

DESIGN / 04

Building a believable world

World design combines visual detail, sound, rules, interaction and technical systems into a coherent experience.
