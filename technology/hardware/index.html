THE DIGITAL OBSERVATORY/HARDWARE & COMPUTER HISTORY

SUBJECT AREA / 01 — MACHINES & ARCHITECTURE

The machines
beneath the surface.

From processors and memory to the home computers that changed everyday life, hardware is the physical foundation of computing.

Inside the system

PROCESSOR

The CPU fetches and executes instructions. Cores, clock speed and architecture influence how work is performed.

MEMORY

Cache and RAM provide fast working space for active programs and data.

STORAGE

Cassette, floppy disk, hard drives, SSDs and flash retain information when power is off.

GRAPHICS

Display adapters and GPUs turn data into pixels, from character screens to 3D environments.

MOTHERBOARD

Chipsets, buses, sockets and firmware connect and coordinate the components.

INPUT & OUTPUT

Keyboards, mice, sound, displays, printers and ports connect people and devices.

From power-on to a working computer

Firmware initialises essential hardware and locates a bootable device. The processor begins executing instructions, memory provides a working area, and storage supplies the operating system and applications. These parts work as a system: performance depends on the balance between them, not one headline specification.

The personal-computing story

Affordable microcomputers brought programming, games and experimentation into homes during the late 1970s and 1980s. British machines from Sinclair, Acorn and Amstrad formed part of a wider international story that also included Commodore, Atari, IBM and Apple. The move from 8-bit micros to 16-bit systems and modern PCs involved changes in memory, graphics, sound, storage and software as well as processors.
