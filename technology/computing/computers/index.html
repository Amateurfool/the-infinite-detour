COMPUTING / PROFILE COLLECTION 01

A generation
of ideas.

A guided journey through the home computers that changed everyday life. We begin with the inventive, varied 8-bit era — from familiar British favourites to machines made around the world — then move into the 16-bit generation and the more powerful systems that followed.

01 / THE 8-BIT GENERATION

When a computer came home.

Many families met computing through these machines. They were gateways to programming, education, games, music and a new kind of creative hobby.

From the ZX80 and ZX81 to the many Spectrum generations and variants, with the QL as a bridge to a different era.

02 / THE 16-BIT GENERATION

More power, new possibilities.

Richer graphics, larger memories, multitasking and new creative tools. The move to 16-bit computing was not one single path: different manufacturers took it in very different directions.

FAMILY 07 / COMMODORE

Commodore Amiga

From the A1000 and A500 to the expandable A2000 and later A1200, exploring custom chips, graphics, sound, Workbench and creative media.

Explore the Amiga family ↗ FAMILY 08 / ATARI

Atari ST

The 520ST and 1040ST, later STE models, TT and Falcon: a family remembered for its value, GEM desktop and built-in MIDI ports.

Explore the Atari ST family ↗ FAMILY 09 / APPLE

Apple Macintosh

From the compact original Macintosh and 512K to the Plus, SE and Macintosh II, exploring the graphical desktop, publishing and design.

Explore the Macintosh family ↗ FAMILY 10 / IBM & COMPATIBLES

The IBM PC and its descendants

The original PC 5150, XT and AT, and the compatible ecosystem that established the x86 PC as a lasting home and business platform.

Explore the IBM PC family ↗ FAMILY 11 / AMSTRAD

Amstrad PC1512 and PC1640

Affordable IBM-compatible systems that brought DOS computing to more UK homes and small businesses, with bundled monitors, mouse and software.

03 / BEYOND THE 16-BIT LABEL

Bridges and new architectures.

Some important machines do not fit neatly into a simple 8-bit or 16-bit story. Their architecture deserves a little explanation rather than an inaccurate label.

TRANSITION / SINCLAIRPLANNED

Sinclair QL

A distinctive 68008-based machine with a 32-bit internal architecture and 8-bit external data bus: a fascinating step away from the familiar 8-bit micros.

1984 · 68008 architecture

The ARM-based Archimedes family and RISC OS: a British design with a different approach to processor architecture, performance and graphical computing.
