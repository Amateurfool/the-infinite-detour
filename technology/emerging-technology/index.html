SUBJECT AREA / 07 — WHAT COMES NEXT

The next set of
possibilities.

Not every announcement becomes a useful product. This part of the observatory separates established capabilities from active development and speculation.

Promising fields, different levels of maturity

AI & MACHINE LEARNING

Systems that learn patterns and generative models that produce text, images, audio and code. Reliability, energy, privacy and governance remain important questions.

QUANTUM COMPUTING

Quantum processors explore particular classes of problems; they are not simply faster replacements for ordinary computers.

PROCESSOR ARCHITECTURE

Chiplets, heterogeneous computing, specialised accelerators and open instruction sets are changing system design.

ROBOTICS & AUTONOMY

Robots combine sensing, control, planning and mechanical design. Safe autonomy depends on robust perception and decisions.

MEMORY & STORAGE

New technologies aim to improve speed, density, endurance and energy efficiency.

FUTURE INTERFACES

Spatial interfaces, wearables and neural interfaces explore alternatives to screens and keyboards.

Three useful status labels

Established: deployed in real products or services, with known capabilities and limitations.

Developing: working prototypes or early products exist, but performance, cost, scale or reliability are still changing.

Experimental: research-stage ideas whose practical usefulness and timelines remain uncertain.

A sceptical kind of curiosity

Ask what problem a technology solves, what trade-offs it introduces, who benefits, what infrastructure it requires and what evidence supports the claims. Distinguish a research result from a product announcement, and a demonstration from a system that works reliably at scale.
